# [42. Trapping Rain Water](https://leetcode.com/problems/trapping-rain-water/)


Given n non-negative integers representing an elevation map where the width of each bar is 1, compute how much water it can trap after raining.

 

**Example 1:**

![[trapping-rain-water-example-1.svg]]

```
Input: height = [0,1,0,2,1,0,1,3,2,1,2,1]
Output: 6
Explanation: The above elevation map (black section) is represented by array [0,1,0,2,1,0,1,3,2,1,2,1]. 
In this case, 6 units of rain water (blue section) are being trapped.
```
**Example 2:**

![[trapping-rain-water-example-2.svg]]

```
Input: height = [4,2,0,3,2,5]
Output: 9
```

Constraints:

- n == height.length
- 1 <= n <= 2 * 10<sup>4</sup>
- 0 <= height[i] <= 10<sup>5</sup>

---

## Solution

### Intuition
Water sitting above any bar is capped by the shorter of the tallest wall to its left and the tallest wall to its right, minus the bar's own height. The clever part is realizing that with [[0.TwoPointerGuide|Two Pointer]], whichever side currently has the smaller running max is the side whose answer is already fully determined, so we can commit its water and step inward.

### Approach 1: Prefix max arrays (for contrast)
```python
# leftMax[i] = tallest bar at or left of i; rightMax[i] = tallest at or right of i
water = sum(max(0, min(leftMax[i], rightMax[i]) - height[i]) for i in range(n))
```
Correct and O(n) time, but it costs O(n) extra space for the two arrays. The two-pointer version removes that. See [[Prefix Sum]] for the running-max idea.

### Approach 2: Two pointers with running maxima
Keep `left`/`right` pointers and `l_max`/`r_max` running maxima. Whenever `l_max <= r_max`, the left bar is bounded by `l_max` (the right side guarantees at least `r_max >= l_max`), so we can safely add its trapped water and advance left. Otherwise do the mirror on the right.

```python
from typing import List

class Solution:
    def trap(self, height: List[int]) -> int:
        if not height:
            return 0
        left, right = 0, len(height) - 1
        l_max, r_max = height[left], height[right]
        total = 0
        while left < right:
            if l_max <= r_max:
                left += 1
                l_max = max(l_max, height[left])
                total += l_max - height[left]   # always >= 0 since l_max just updated
            else:
                right -= 1
                r_max = max(r_max, height[right])
                total += r_max - height[right]
        return total
```

> The invariant `l_max <= r_max` guarantees the right side has a wall at least as tall, so `l_max` alone bounds the water at the left bar. That is why we never need the actual right max at that position.

**Water trapped at each index equals `min(left_max, right_max) - height`, capped at 0 (`height = [4,2,0,3,2,5]`):**

| i | height[i] | left_max | right_max | min(left,right) | water |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 0 |
| 1 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| 2 | 0 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 4 |
| 3 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 1 |
| 4 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 0 |

Total water = 9

### Walkthrough
Tracing `height = [4,2,0,3,2,5]`:

| left | right | l_max | r_max | side | added | total |
|------|-------|-------|-------|------|-------|-------|
| 0 | 5 | 4 | 5 | left (4<=5) | move left to 1, l_max=4, +4-2=2 | 2 |
| 1 | 5 | 4 | 5 | left | move left to 2, l_max=4, +4-0=4 | 6 |
| 2 | 5 | 4 | 5 | left | move left to 3, l_max=4, +4-3=1 | 7 |
| 3 | 5 | 4 | 5 | left | move left to 4, l_max=4, +4-2=2 | 9 |
| 4 | 5 | 4 | 5 | left | move left to 5, l_max=5, +5-5=0 | 9 |
| 5 | 5 | - | - | stop | - | 9 |

Final output: **9**.

### Complexity
- **Time:** O(n). Each bar is visited exactly once as the pointers converge.
- **Space:** O(1). Only pointers and two running maxima, improving on the array approach.

### Key concepts to review
- [[0.TwoPointerGuide|Two Pointer]] - the smaller running max determines which side to settle first.
- [[Prefix Sum]] - the running-max precomputation behind the simpler array approach.
- [[04.ContainerWithMostWater]] - sibling where the shorter wall again drives the decision.

### Common pitfalls
- Updating the running max after computing water instead of before, which can add negative amounts.
- Using `left <= right` with index access that re-counts a bar; `left < right` with pre-increment avoids it.
- Forgetting the empty-input guard before reading `height[0]`.
